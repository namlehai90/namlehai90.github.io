I’m **Nam H. Le**, a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Vermont working on the intersection of **evolutionary computation**, **collective intelligence**, and **language-based control systems**. My research focuses on how intelligent behavior emerges in complex adaptive systems — from evolving neural networks and autonomous swarms to simulated multicellular collectives.

Previously, I held a research fellowship at the University of Southampton (UK) and earned my Ph.D. in Evolutionary Computation at University College Dublin (Ireland). I work closely with **Josh Bongard** and **Michael Levin** on projects that bridge AI, artificial life, and developmental biology.

🔍 **My research interests include**:
- Neuroevolution, self-teaching architectures, and LLM-based controllers
- Swarm intelligence and multi-agent reinforcement learning
- Open-ended evolution and the emergence of collective intelligence

🧪 Recent projects include:
- **ZapGPT** – Using LLMs to linguistically control simulated cellular behavior
- **VitaNova** – An artificial life platform simulating the evolution of collective reproduction
- **Self-taught Networks** – Combining evolution with individual and social learning strategies
